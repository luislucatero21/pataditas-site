Pataditas / Apple Watch kick counter

Baby kick counter for Apple Watch: count kicks from your wrist

By Luis Angel Lucatero Villanueva, founder of Kaelux, who makes Pataditas · Updated November 24, 2026

If your baby is moving less than usual, has stopped moving or the pattern has changed, contact your midwife, doctor or maternity unit now, day or night. Do not wait until tomorrow, and do not rely on an app, a kick count or a home heartbeat monitor to decide. If you cannot reach them, go to the nearest hospital. This page is general information, not medical advice.

Pataditas is a baby kick counter app for iPhone with a companion Apple Watch app. This page explains what the watch app does and what it cannot do. It describes Pataditas 1.5.

Quick answer

  • Apple Watch does not detect your baby’s movements by itself: you tap the watch each time you feel one.
  • In Pataditas, a tap on the baby illustration logs a kick; Roll and Hiccup sit below it, and Undo fixes a mistaken tap.
  • A ring shows your progress toward the goal (10 movements in 2 hours by default), and complications put it on your watch face or in the Smart Stack.
  • Entries sync with the iPhone through your private iCloud. You need the iPhone app, iOS 26 and watchOS 26 or later. Counting is free.
  • If your baby moves less than usual, contact your midwife, doctor or maternity unit now. Do not wait for the app.

Can Apple Watch track baby kicks by itself?

No. Apple Watch does not detect your baby’s movements by itself, and neither does Pataditas. You feel each movement and tap the watch; Pataditas saves the time and the type of movement.

How do I count kicks on Apple Watch with Pataditas?

Open Pataditas on your watch and tap the baby illustration each time you feel a kick. The watch gives a short tap on your wrist and the count goes up. Use Roll or Hiccup below the illustration for those movements, and Undo at the top if you tapped by mistake. There is no session to start or stop.

What does the watch show while I count?

A ring around the baby fills as you get closer to your goal: by default 10 movements within 2 hours, shown as “of 10 in 2 h” next to your count. When you reach it, the watch shows “Goal reached!” with how long it took. Turn the Digital Crown or swipe to the next screen for today: your count, hiccups listed separately, the last 2 hours, the time of the last movement and a line of the day’s movements.

Patterns and the PDF report stay on the iPhone. The 10-in-2-hours goal is the app’s own grouping, not a medical threshold.

Can I add it to my watch face or the Smart Stack?

Yes. Pataditas offers circular, rectangular, inline and corner complications, which you can also use in the Smart Stack. The circular one shows your count in the last 2 hours inside a progress ring, with a check mark once you reach the goal. Tapping a complication opens Pataditas.

Does it sync with the iPhone, and does it work when my iPhone isn’t nearby?

Yes. Each movement is saved on the watch itself and syncs with the iPhone app through your private iCloud, so you can log even when your iPhone isn’t with you. When the watch and the iPhone are connected, movements also go straight between them, so they show up sooner.

Can I log a roll or hiccup from the watch?

Yes. Roll and Hiccup sit just below the baby illustration. Hiccups are saved in your history but don’t count toward the goal, the same as on the iPhone.

Can my partner use Pataditas on Apple Watch?

If your partner follows your movements through partner sharing and their iPhone shows your shared movements, their watch shows today’s count, read-only, and does not record anything.

What do I need?

An iPhone with iOS 26 or later and the Pataditas iPhone app, and an Apple Watch with watchOS 26 or later. The Watch app works together with the iPhone app, which you download from the App Store; you don’t need an account.

Is the Apple Watch kick counter free?

Yes. Counting is free on iPhone and Apple Watch. Pataditas Plus is optional and adds pattern charts, the PDF report, your full history and sharing with your partner; see the prices on the home page.

What if my baby moves less than usual?

Contact your midwife, doctor or maternity unit immediately if movements slow down, stop or change. Do not wait for a counting goal or until tomorrow. The NHS says to call straight away, even in the middle of the night, and not to rely on a home heartbeat listening device.
