Why no sessions?

Most kick counters ask you to press “start”, watch the clock and press “stop”. But babies don’t move on a schedule, and you shouldn’t have to hold your phone like a stopwatch.

In Pataditas you simply tap when you feel a kick or a roll. The app groups your taps into a count on its own and notices when you reach 10 movements within 2 hours — then celebrates with you, softly.

Fewer buttons, less pressure, more time to enjoy the little feet.

Questions about counting baby kicks and fetal movements

Quick answers about what the NHS and Count the Kicks say, and about Pataditas. General information, not medical advice.

How do you count baby kicks?

Count the Kicks suggests picking a time of day when your baby is usually active, sitting with your feet up or lying on your side, and noting each movement until you reach 10. With Pataditas you simply tap whenever you feel a kick, roll or swish; there is nothing to start or stop, and the app keeps the count and the time for you. The NHS says you do not need to count, so ask your midwife or doctor which approach they recommend.

How many kicks (fetal movements) are normal?

There is no single normal number. The NHS says there is no set number of movements you should feel each day; what matters is getting to know your baby’s usual pattern. Count the Kicks instead suggests timing how long it takes to feel 10 movements, at about the same time each day, from week 28. Whatever you do, contact your midwife, doctor or maternity unit straight away if the pattern changes.

When do babies start kicking, and when should I start counting?

Most people first feel movements between about 16 and 24 weeks (NHS); in a first pregnancy it can be after 20 weeks. Count the Kicks suggests starting daily counts at week 28, or week 26 if your pregnancy is higher risk or you are expecting more than one baby, while the NHS says counting is not required. If you have not felt movement by 24 weeks, tell your midwife. Ask your midwife or doctor what they recommend for you.

Do the NHS and Count the Kicks agree on counting fetal movements?

Guidance on counting differs. Count the Kicks (USA) suggests timing how long it takes to feel 10 movements, starting at week 28 (week 26 if you are higher risk or expecting more than one baby). The NHS (UK) says there is no set number of movements and you do not need to count kicks, but you should get to know your baby’s usual pattern. Both agree on the key point: if your baby moves less than usual, stops moving, or the pattern changes, contact your midwife, doctor or maternity unit straight away. The NHS adds that you should call even in the middle of the night and not wait until the next day. Pataditas only records what you feel; ask your care team which approach they recommend.

Do hiccups count as kicks?

No. Count the Kicks does not count hiccups, because it describes them as involuntary. In Pataditas you can log hiccups as their own type: they’re saved in your history but don’t count toward the 10 movements. If you are unsure whether you felt a hiccup or a movement, ask your midwife or doctor.

What does “10 movements in 2 hours” mean?

Pataditas groups 10 logged kicks or rolls within two hours. This is the app’s own grouping: it is not a health assessment or a universal daily target. Follow your care team’s guidance.

What should I do if my baby moves less than usual?

Contact your midwife, doctor or maternity unit immediately if movements slow down, stop or change. Do not wait for a counting goal or until tomorrow. The NHS says to call straight away, even in the middle of the night, and not to rely on a home heartbeat listening device. NHS.

How does Pataditas compare today with earlier entries?

After seven days of entries, Pataditas shows how the movements you logged today compare with your earlier days. Missing entries can change the comparison. It cannot measure all movement or tell whether your baby is well, and it is not a medical assessment.

Is my data private?

Yes. There is no account, no ads and no tracking, and anonymous usage statistics are optional and off unless you turn them on. Your data stays on your iPhone and in your own private iCloud (CloudKit). The developer cannot see it. Read the full privacy policy.

Does it work offline? What does iCloud do?

Yes, you can log movements without a connection. If you’re signed in to iCloud, your data syncs privately between your devices through your own iCloud account, and your settings (like your name and due date) sync through iCloud key-value storage. Neither is visible to us.

Entries sync with the iPhone app through your private iCloud. It works together with the iPhone app, which you download from the App Store.
